# Day 204 - Media ethics terminology

## Lesson

"Media ethics terminology" at this advanced level is about providing you with even more precise terms needed to engage in sophisticated discussions about how ethical principles are *upheld*, *challenged*, and *rectified* within media organizations and the broader information ecosystem. This goes beyond understanding general principles to encompass concrete qualities, institutional responses, and grave breaches of journalistic trust.

Think of it as having an even finer set of precision tools to dissect the ethical framework and conduct of media, focusing on the very fiber of professional integrity and the mechanisms for maintaining it. You'll learn specific terms for unwavering uprightness, consistent trustworthiness, formal complaint resolution, external influences, and outright deception.

Here are the key vocabulary and phrases you'll learn today:

### 1. Core Ethical Qualities:

*   **`اسْتِقَامَةٌ`** (istiqāmatun) - 'is-ti-qaa-ma-tun' (feminine noun)
    *   *Meaning:* Integrity, rectitude, uprightness.
    *   *Explanation:* This noun refers to the quality of being honest and having strong moral principles; moral uprightness. It's a foundational ethical virtue for any journalist or media organization.
        *   *Example:* `تَعْتَبِرُ الْجُمْهُورُ الِاسْتِقَامَةَ أَهَمَّ قِيَمِ الصِّحَافَةِ.` (The public considers integrity the most important value of journalism.)
*   **`مَوْثُوقِيَّةٌ`** (mawthūqiyyatun) - 'maw-thoo-qiy-ya-tun' (feminine noun)
    *   *Meaning:* Reliability, trustworthiness.
    *   *Explanation:* This noun refers to the quality of being consistently good in performance or accuracy; able to be relied on or trusted. It builds on `مِصْدَاقِيَّةٌ` (credibility, Day 159) by emphasizing consistent and verifiable dependability.
        *   *Example:* `تُعَزِّزُ الشَّفَافِيَّةُ مَوْثُوقِيَّةَ الْمَصَادِرِ.` (Transparency enhances the reliability of sources.)

### 2. Institutional Accountability Mechanisms:

*   **`لَجْنَةٌ`** (lajnatun) - 'laj-na-tun' (feminine noun)
    *   *Meaning:* Committee.
    *   *Explanation:* This term refers to a group of people appointed for a specific function, typically consisting of members of a larger group. In media ethics, these committees often oversee standards or investigate complaints.
    *   *Plural:* **`لِجَانٌ`** (lijānun) - 'li-jaan' (feminine plural, broken plural, diptote).
        *   *Example phrase:* **`لَجْنَةٌ أَخْلَاقِيَّةٌ`** (lajnatun akhlāqiyyatun) - 'laj-na-tun akh-laa-qiy-ya-tun' (feminine noun + adjective) - Ethical committee.
*   **`تَصْحِيحٌ`** (taṣḥīḥun) - 'taṣ-ḥee-ḥun' (masculine Masdar, Form II)
    *   *Meaning:* Correction, rectification.
    *   *Explanation:* This refers to the act of correcting an error or making something right. It's a crucial part of media accountability when factual inaccuracies are published.
    *   *Verb form:* **`يُصَحِّحُ`** (yuṣaḥḥiḥu) - 'yu-ṣaḥ-ḥi-ḥu' (present tense verb, Form II) - He corrects. Its feminine form is `تُصَحِّحُ` (tuṣaḥḥiḥu).
        *   *Example:* `يَجِبُ عَلَى الْإِعْلَامِ نَشْرُ تَصْحِيحٍ فَوَراً.` (The media must publish a correction immediately.)

### 3. Challenges to Integrity:

*   **`ضَغْطٌ`** (ḍaghṭun) - 'ḍaghṭ' (masculine noun)
    *   *Meaning:* Pressure.
    *   *Explanation:* This refers to the use of persuasion, influence, or intimidation to make someone do something. Media organizations often face various `ضُغُوطٌ` (plural) that can compromise their independence or ethical standards.
    *   *Plural:* **`ضُغُوطٌ`** (ḍughūṭun) - 'ḍu-ghooṭ' (feminine plural noun).
        *   *Example:* `تُوَاجِهُ الْقَنَاةُ ضُغُوطًا سِيَاسِيَّةً.` (The channel faces political pressures.)
*   **`تَلَاعُبٌ`** (talāʿubun) - 'ta-laa-ʿu-bun' (masculine Masdar, Form VI)
    *   *Meaning:* Manipulation, tampering.
    *   *Explanation:* This noun refers to the skillful handling or control of something or someone, especially in a devious or unfair way. It implies a deliberate and often dishonest alteration.
        *   *Example phrase:* **`تَلَاعُبٌ بِالْحَقَائِقِ`** (talāʿubun bi-l-ḥaqāʾiqi) - 'ta-laa-ʿu-bun bi-l-ḥa-qaa-ʾiq' (masculine Masdar + prepositional phrase) - Manipulation of facts. This describes the severe ethical breach of intentionally distorting or fabricating factual information. (`حَقَائِقُ` is the plural of `حَقِيقَةٌ` - fact, Day 172).

By integrating `اسْتِقَامَةٌ`, `مَوْثُوقِيَّةٌ`, `لَجْنَةٌ أَخْلَاقِيَّةٌ`, `تَصْحِيحٌ`, `ضُغُوطٌ`, and `تَلَاعُبٌ بِالْحَقَائِقِ` with your existing advanced vocabulary, argumentation structures, and rhetorical phrases, you will be able to discuss and analyze media ethics with unparalleled clarity, depth, and the highest level of journalistic and academic sophistication.
